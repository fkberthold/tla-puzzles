# Requirement 2: nobody is off both rolls

At every moment, every member holds a whole entry on at least one of the two
rolls.

## Satisfies it

`allowed.md`, both runs. At every state each member holds a whole entry on one
roll or the other. The partway refusal is the case worth reading twice. m2
never goes over, and her sending entry carries her right through the refusal
and after it.

## Violates it

A model that strikes the sending entry first and enters the member on the
receiving roll in a later step.

| # | sending | receiving | answer | step |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | m1 on, m2 on | m1 off, m2 off | waiting | the instruction is in the clerk's hands |
| 2 | m1 off, m2 on | m1 off, m2 off | waiting | the clerk strikes m1's sending entry before writing her onto the receiving roll |

**Where it breaks:** state 2. m1 is off both rolls. She belongs to no branch,
pays into nothing and draws nothing, and rule 4 calls a clerk who leaves her
there in breach.

Requirements 1 and 3 both hold at state 2. Nothing on either roll is
part-written, and nobody holds two whole entries. Of rule 4's last two
promises, this is the one the orders require rather than ask for.
